Specialties · How we help

EMDR therapy

Eye Movement Desensitization and Reprocessing is a structured therapy that helps the brain process distressing memories, so they feel less present and less charged.

Recommended for PTSD by the American Psychological Association and the World Health Organization.

See EMDR clinicians
Photo: two chairs by a window, soft daylight

Who it helps

EMDR may help if you live with

  • Memories that intrude or replay
  • Panic or startle responses tied to past events
  • The effects of an abusive relationship
  • Childhood experiences that still shape how you feel
  • Grief that feels stuck

The process

What EMDR looks like

  1. 01

    History & planning

    We map what you want to work on, and in what order.

  2. 02

    Preparation

    You learn grounding skills to use in and between sessions.

  3. 03

    Processing

    Short sets of eye movements or taps while you hold a memory in mind.

  4. 04

    Integration

    We check what has shifted and close each session steady.

Questions

About EMDR

Do I have to describe the memory in detail?

No. EMDR does not require you to talk through every detail. You stay in control of what you share.

How many sessions will I need?

It depends on your history and goals. Your clinician will give you an estimate after the assessment.

Can EMDR be done online?

Yes. Online EMDR uses on-screen movement or self-tapping and follows the same structure.
